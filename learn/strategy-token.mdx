ST is the yield side of a [vToken](/learn/vtoken). When a vToken is split, the yield claim becomes ST and the points claim becomes [EPT](/learn/expected-points-token). You don't need to split yourself — buy ST directly on the orderbook with your vTokens.

## What Is ST

ST represents your claim on the vault's USDC value. The vault pays a fixed interest rate, so NAV grows predictably. At maturity, you redeem ST for USDC at the final NAV.

ST trades on the **ST/vToken** orderbook. Yield seekers buy ST at a discount to NAV. The discount exists because points farmers buying EPT create ST supply on the other side of the orderbook.

---

## The Yield

Yield comes from a fixed-rate loan. The market maker borrows vault capital and pays back a fixed annual rate.

| Term | Example |
|---|---|
| Gross APR | 15% |
| Performance fee | 20% of returns |
| Net APR | 12% |

ST holders earn the net APR on their position. They do **not** earn credits or points — that exposure stays with vToken and [EPT](/learn/expected-points-token) holders.

---

## Buy the Discount

Points farmers buying EPT push ST prices below NAV. That discount is the yield seeker's opportunity.

**Example.** NAV is 1.00. ST trades at \$0.98. The vault pays 12% net APR. Over a 60-day epoch:

- Discount capture: 2%
- Yield: 12% × 60/365 ≈ 2%
- **Total: \~4% per epoch, or \~24% annualized**

The deeper the discount, the higher the effective return. The discount acts as a buffer — the only way you lose money is if something goes wrong with the vault itself, not from market movements.

---

## Redemption

After the epoch matures:

$$\text{USDC received} = \text{your ST} \times \text{final NAV}$$

Your ST is burned. No fee on redemption.

---

## Selling Before Maturity

Sell ST on the orderbook at any time during the active epoch. You receive vTokens. This is a market sale, not redemption — you get whatever price the market offers.

As the epoch approaches maturity, ST price tends to converge toward NAV.

---

## What Drives ST Price

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| EPT demand | More EPT buying creates more ST supply, widening the discount |
| Yield seeker demand | Absorbs ST supply, narrowing the discount |
| Time to maturity | Price converges toward NAV as the epoch ends |
| Vault NAV | Higher NAV means higher redemption value |

The ST discount reflects the cost of points. When points are in high demand, farmers buy EPT aggressively, ST supply grows, and the discount widens. Yield seekers absorb that supply and earn a higher effective return.
