EPT price depends on one equation. This page walks through that equation and what drives it.

## The Split Parity

ST and EPT are the two components of a vToken. Their prices are locked:

$$\text{ST price} + \text{EPT price} = \text{vToken price}$$

vToken trades at or near NAV (since you can always mint or burn at NAV). So if ST trades at price $X$:

$$\text{EPT}_{\text{fair}} = \text{NAV} - X$$

If ST trades at \$0.98 and NAV is 1.00, EPT fair value is \$0.02.

**Enforcement.** This isn't just a formula — arbitrage enforces it. If ST + EPT prices drift above vToken price, anyone can split vTokens and sell the components. If they drift below, anyone can buy both and recombine. Both directions work, so pricing stays tight.

Manual splitting and recombining mechanics are under review and may be permissioned at launch. Arbitrage enforcement may initially operate through permissioned actors.

## Returns Framing

The natural question is "is \$0.02 too expensive?" The better question: **what is the implied return?**

EPT is a claim on future points. Its value depends on how many points you earn and what those points are worth at TGE.

### Worked Example

You use 5 vTokens to buy 250 EPT at \$0.02 each during a 60-day epoch:

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Buy** | 250 EPT for 5 vTokens (\$5 at NAV) |
| **Hold** | You accrue credits over the epoch. Your credit share works out to 50 points. |
| **Claim** | Epoch matures. You claim 50 PointsTokens (after 25% PointsToken claim fee). |
| **TGE** | Each point is worth \$0.20 at TGE. You burn 50 PointsTokens for \$10. |
| **Return** | \$10 out on \$5 in = **100% return over 60 days** |

### Same Price, Different Return

If EPT is \$0.02 with 8 weeks left, you earn credits for all 8 weeks. If EPT is still \$0.02 with 2 weeks left, you earn credits for just 2 weeks. Same price, very different return.

In practice, EPT price decays as the epoch progresses because fewer credits remain to be earned. This is the market correctly pricing a shrinking earning window — not a mechanical decay curve.

### Leverage Is Not a Fixed Number

At \$0.02 per EPT, 100 vTokens buys 5,000 EPT — 50x more than holding vTokens. But that "50x" means very different things depending on when you buy. Early in the epoch, those EPT accrue credits for weeks. Late, they accrue for days. Same leverage, vastly different APR.

The UI shows both **APR** and **leverage** side by side. Limit orders are placed in APR terms — the price adjusts automatically as time passes.

## Orderbook Mechanics

One orderbook, two pairs: **ST/vToken** and **EPT/vToken**. All orders are denominated in vTokens.

### How Orders Match

| Scenario | What happens |
|---|---|
| EPT buyer + EPT seller | Secondary trade. vTokens ↔ EPT. Seller keeps credits. Buyer starts from zero. |
| ST buyer + ST seller | Secondary trade. vTokens ↔ ST. |
| EPT buyer + ST buyer | Both contribute vTokens. Engine splits them into ST + EPT. Delivers each side. |
| EPT seller + ST seller | ST + EPT recombined into vTokens. Distributed between sellers by matched price. |

### APR-Based Orders

Users don't set raw prices. They set APR constraints:

- **ST buyers** set the minimum APR they require
- **EPT buyers** set the maximum APR they're willing to pay

The engine translates APR to executable prices based on time remaining. APR is time-invariant — "I need at least X%" stays the same regardless of when in the epoch you place the order.

## What Drives EPT Price

| Factor | Effect on EPT Price |
|---|---|
| **Time remaining** | More time = more credits to earn = higher price |
| **Expected points value** | Higher expected TGE value = higher price |
| **ST discount** | ST and EPT linked through split parity |
| **Points demand** | More buyers on EPT orderbook = higher price |
| **Vault NAV** | Higher NAV = higher vToken price = more room for EPT value |

---

## What Drives the ST Discount

The ST discount is the other side of EPT pricing.

### Factors That Widen the Discount

| Factor | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Strong EPT demand** | More EPT buying → more order matching → more ST supply hitting the market |
| **Early in epoch** | Maximum time until maturity = more uncertainty |

### Factors That Narrow the Discount

| Factor | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Yield seeker demand** | Buyers seeking fixed returns absorb ST supply |
| **Approaching maturity** | Less time risk remaining, price converges to NAV |

### Equilibrium

Points farmers want cheap EPT, which requires a wide ST discount. Yield seekers want cheap ST, which the wide discount provides. The market clears where the marginal points farmer's willingness to pay meets the marginal yield seeker's willingness to hold.

## Worked Example (\$100)

**Setup:** NAV = 1.00, ST trading at \$0.98, EPT at \$0.02.

### Points Farmer

Buy EPT with 100 vTokens at \$0.02 per EPT:

$$\frac{100 \text{ vTokens}}{0.02 \text{ vTokens per EPT}} = 5{,}000\text{ EPT}$$

Compare: holding 100 vTokens earns credits on 100 tokens. Buying EPT earns credits on **5,000 tokens** — 50x points leverage. The leverage comes from the price. But remember: [same leverage, different APR depending on timing](#leverage-is-not-a-fixed-number).

### Yield Seeker

Buy ST with 100 vTokens at \$0.98 per ST. The vault pays 12% net APR. Over a 60-day epoch:

- Discount capture: 2%
- Yield: 12% × 60/365 ≈ 2%
- **Total: \~4% per epoch, or \~24% annualized**

### Where the Value Goes

| Participant | Pays | Gets | Exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Points farmer | 100 vTokens | 5,000 EPT | 50x leveraged points |
| Yield seeker | 100 vTokens | \~102 ST | Fixed yield + discount |
| vToken holder | 100 USDC | 100 vTokens | Both yield + points at 1x |

We don't create value. We separate it.
